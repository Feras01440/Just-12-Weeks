# Recovery Pattern Comparison — the Signature Experience

**Status:** exploratory. Recovery is treated as the product’s strongest differentiator candidate. This document compares how each direction expresses the six recovery situations, records the shared covenant, and assesses whether recovery can become a recognisable brand signature.

**Evidence base** (research brief §5): one missed day does not impair habit formation (Lally 2010); *displayed* broken streaks measurably reduce continuation (Silverman & Barasch 2022); lapse-cascades are driven by internal-stable attribution and guilt (Marlatt AVE); temporal landmarks are natural re-entry doors (Dai/Milkman/Riis 2014); autonomy support beats compulsion (SDT).

## The recovery covenant (identical across directions)

Recovery never: shames · blames · displays a broken streak · erases earned progress · demands an explanation · forces a restart · pretends nothing happened · manipulates through loss.
Recovery always: acknowledges plainly (one line) · re-anchors to what is kept · offers the smallest honest next step first · gives the user the capacity decision · keeps the full-schedule option one tap away · treats rescheduling as honourable.

“Pretends nothing happened” is the subtle one: each direction *marks* missed time truthfully (em-dash / NO READING / a gap in the weave) — without deficit styling. The record is honest; the tone is unbothered.

## The six situations, three ways

| Situation | A · Quarto — “the bookmark never moves backwards” | B · Meridian — “recalibration, not reproach” | C · Atelier — “visible mending” |
|---|---|---|---|
| One missed day | “A quiet day.” The day becomes an em-dash in the margin. Primary: *Pick up today*. Lighter form offered, counts in full. | Log shows “— NO READING —” as an ordinary entry. “The rail is where you left it.” Primary: *Take today’s reading*. | A one-pick space in the band. “A space in the cloth, not a hole in the week.” Primary: *Pick up the thread*. |
| Several missed days | “The book kept your place.” Weeks I–II shown intact (“kept”). Either/or: rejoin as planned / step back in gently. | “No readings for three days” stated once; two re-entry options as physical controls. The rail never rewinds. | The gap span visible; “the cloth holds.” Two soft options: rejoin / gentle re-entry. |
| Long absence | “Where you left off” — reopening a book: ribbon, last thing done, three capacity choices, revised-edition note. | RECALIBRATE: the capacity dial (Low / Steady / Full) — the signature interaction; the instrument recomputes today. | TIE BACK ON: the woven cloth shown whole (“what you wove is woven”), knot a new thread, three capacities. |
| Returning with lower capacity | The gentler form is a first-class page (“counts in full” pull-quote). | Capacity dial at LOW arms a ten-minute reading. | The lighter thread — thinner pick, same row. |
| Returning with renewed motivation | The further form exists but is framed as variation, not target. | Dial at FULL simply resumes; the instrument does not applaud excess. | The further thread; the cloth doesn’t need heroics. |
| Original schedule no longer realistic | “Revised edition” — the remaining chapters reprint over more weeks; framed as an edition, not a downgrade. | “Re-index the traverse” — same rail, wider spacing between detents. | “Re-warp the loom” — more rows, same cloth; the finished band is simply longer. |

## Assessment

**Which recovery is most ownable?** C’s *visible mending* is the strongest brand-signature candidate: it has a physical-world referent with exactly the right moral (sashiko — the repair is part of the object’s beauty), it appears in the persistent UI (the mend stitch stays in the band and in the completion artefact), and no competitor pattern resembles it. Its risk is comprehension-on-first-sight, mitigated by the caption pairing rule.

**Most immediately understandable?** A’s bookmark. Everyone has put a book down for three weeks; nobody apologises to a book. Weakest at *displaying* recovery later (an em-dash is honest but almost invisible — deliberately so).

**Most empowering interaction?** B’s capacity dial — it converts the awkward “how do I come back?” moment into operating a control, which reframes the user as the operator rather than the offender. It is also the most transplantable: any direction could adopt a capacity choice on return (all three already ask it in `long-absence`).

**Recommendation for post-sprint testing** (not a selection): test C’s mend and B’s dial with returning users specifically — recovery comprehension after a 3+ week gap is the scenario that matters, and it is the one synthetic critique can least stand in for.
