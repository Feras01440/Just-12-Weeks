# The Week-12 Completion Experience

**Status:** Draft for Gate 4. The promise's payoff — "a valuable completion experience" is a founder requirement from the original brief, and the moment the brand's whole claim ("actually finishing") becomes tangible. Also the product's most shareable artefact, earned honestly.

## 1. Intent

Completion must feel like **arriving somewhere real**: proportionate ceremony, honest evidence, and a considered answer to "so what now?" — in that order. It is designed for three audiences at once: the completer (meaning), their future self (the keepable report), and — only by the user's explicit choice — their world (the share).

## 2. The arc (three movements)

### 2.1 The finish (the moment)
The final guided action is framed as what it is from the moment it appears ("the last one"). On completion: the **finishing moment** — the signature visual object (Stage 3 candidate: the twelve-part accumulation completing) resolves. Weight, not noise: this is the one place in the product where celebration is unrestrained *in feeling* while still calm *in craft*. Reduced-motion: a fully-designed still resolution of equal dignity (not a skipped animation). Duration: seconds, skippable, never gating the report.

### 2.2 The report (the substance)
Assembled from what actually happened (evidence model §7), narrated in the programme's voice:
1. **The goal, in their words** — the sentence they wrote at the start, given back.
2. **What they did** — guided work completed, milestones passed, checkpoints demonstrated; concrete counts in human phrasing ("57 guided sessions across 12 weeks"), never percentage-scoring.
3. **What changed** — capability arc (week 1 vs week 12 in the programme's own terms); measurement trends *if the user took them*; artefacts *if they made them*; visual evidence *if they chose it*. Only what exists — absence is invisible, never a gap.
4. **The comeback story** — lapses navigated, re-entries, the restructured weeks: presented as the *strongest* section ("week 7: four days away, and you came back"). This is the section no competitor can fake and the one users will remember.
5. **The honest close** — the programme's outcome statement, claims-policy compliant: what this evidence shows, what twelve weeks can and can't establish, and the reviewer-authored "what tends to matter next".
Formats: in-app (permanent, in Journey), export (PDF/HTML with the same care — FR-34), and a share card (next).

### 2.3 The transition (F23, ⚠ Q13)
From the report, the week-13 conversation: next journey / maintenance / a clean end — all designed with equal respect (the clean-end path's quality *is* brand strategy). No urgency mechanics, ever, here of all places.

## 3. Partial completion (designed, not exceptional)

Challenges ending before the rule is met (user closes it out, or week 12 arrives incomplete) get the same three movements, honestly scaled: a quieter finish acknowledgment ("ten weeks, and here's what stands"), the same evidence-true report structure, the same dignified transition. All-or-nothing is the abandonment machine this product exists to break; the report is where that promise is kept last.

## 4. The share card (explicit-only)

Never prompted before the report is seen; private by default. A composed card (signature visual + goal statement + one fact the user picks) — no body metrics, no photos by default, nothing auto-included. Sharing is the user narrating their own finish, not the app farming acquisition. (`completion_report_shared`, system share sheet only.)

## 5. Second-cycle hooks

The report seeds the next cycle without upsell stink: "journey one" becomes a permanent, revisitable object; a second journey's report will reference it ("your second twelve weeks"). Long-term: the shelf of finished journeys is the product's deepest retention asset — identity, not streaks.

## 6. Acceptance

Stage 5: completers of a simulated journey describe the report as "true / mine / worth keeping" (card sort); zero users describe it as "a certificate" (generic-detector). Report readable end-to-end by screen reader in logical order; export accessible (tagged). The finishing moment passes reduced-motion review as designed-equal. G-series and claims-policy checks apply to every sentence.
