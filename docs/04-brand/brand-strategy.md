# Brand Strategy

**Status:** Draft for Gate 3. Strategy only — no logo, no final visual direction (three territories follow in art-direction-a/b/c; the founder chooses at Gate 3). Constraints inherited: category-neutral language (D-004), adult-inclusive across ages (D-001), calm and honest behaviour (D-002, paywall principles), the anti-generic mandate (founder requirement).

## 1. Brand purpose

**To make finishing normal.** Most people's relationship with meaningful goals is a history of unfinished starts; the brand exists to change what people believe about themselves as finishers — one guided twelve-week proof at a time.

## 2. Brand promise

> **Twelve weeks, honestly guided, actually finished.**

Every brand expression must be redeemable against this: guidance (we tell and show), honesty (no tricks, no inflated claims, no fake urgency), completion (the ending is the point — and we design for the comeback, because finishing includes falling and returning).

## 3. Brand personality

**The brilliant, calm coach who has seen every version of week 5.** Five traits, each with its guard-rail:

| Trait | Meaning | Never becomes |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| Assured | Knows the path; says so plainly | Arrogant, preachy |
| Warm | On your side, especially in the lapse | Saccharine, gushing |
| Precise | Specific numbers, specific steps, honest costs | Cold, clinical |
| Unhurried | Twelve weeks is the tempo; no urgency theatre | Passive, aimless |
| Grown-up | Speaks to adults of every age as equals | Corporate, beige |

## 4. Brand values (operationalised, not posters)

1. **Honesty before conversion** — enforced by claims policy + paywall principles.
2. **The comeback is sacred** — recovery gets the best design in the product; lapsed users are never a segment to be "won back" with pressure.
3. **Evidence over vibes** — named authors, named reviewers, citations users can touch.
4. **Everyone's twelve weeks count** — an 68-year-old's first 5K and a 24-year-old's first app ship with equal dignity (D-001).
5. **Craft is respect** — premium finish because the product asks for twelve weeks of someone's life.

## 5. Desired emotional response

- **Minute one:** "This is serious — and it's kind." (Trust + relief.)
- **Week 5 lapse:** "It gets it. There's a way back." (Absence of shame.)
- **Weekly review:** "I can actually see it moving." (Quiet pride.)
- **Week 12:** "I did this." (Earned, personal, not app-flavoured — the brand recedes at the summit; the user owns the moment.)

## 6. Tone of voice

Fully specified in [`03-ux/content-strategy.md`](../03-ux/content-strategy.md) (voice table, lexicons, surface rules). Brand-level addition: the brand narrates **the user's competence, never its own** ("you built five weeks" not "we helped you build five weeks").

## 7. Words

**We use:** twelve weeks · journey · guided · today · built · standing · way back · clean page · finish · evidence · your pace · chapter · milestone.
**We avoid:** *(pressure/hype/blame lexicons, plus brand-level bans)* hack · grind · crush · beast · optimise yourself · unlock your potential · self-care-industrial vocabulary ("wellness journey" as filler) · gamification vocabulary (points, XP, levels) · "users" in any user-facing copy (people, you).

## 8. Visual principles (territory-independent)

1. **One signature object** carries the twelve-week arc everywhere it appears (the signature-experience decision) — recognisability lives in an *owned mechanic*, not a colour.
2. **Calm surfaces, meaningful ink:** generous space; every visual element informs or orients; decoration that doesn't mean something doesn't ship.
3. **Typography does the talking** — the product is words and guidance; type is the primary brand carrier, imagery secondary.
4. **Accessible is the aesthetic:** contrast, scale-tolerance and reduced-motion variants designed as first-class expressions, not compliance shadows (D-001).
5. **The brand recedes as the user advances:** week-12 surfaces foreground the user's evidence, not our mark.
6. **Category-neutral chrome:** universal surfaces carry no goal-domain imagery; programmes bring their own world within the system (D-004/PRQ-01).

## 9. Anti-principles *(the founder's banned list, made brand law)*

No default Inter/Poppins/Manrope/Montserrat identity · no purple-blue SaaS gradients · no glassmorphism · no oversized-rounded-card samey layouts · no decorative blobs/random 3D · no confetti as a personality · no stock aspiration photography (sunrise silhouettes, laughing salad) · no fake testimonials/statistics/scarcity ever · no mascot *(considered and rejected: mascots skew the age range and soften the coach authority — Finch's pattern is right for Finch, wrong here)*.

## 10. Differentiation (vs the field's visual centres of gravity)

The audit mapped four occupied aesthetics: gamified-cute, glossy-celebrity, utility-chart, institutional-warm — plus Sleep Cycle's calm-instrument (admired, but attached to a shrinking single-purpose product). **The open position: the serious-warm guide — editorial/instrumental gravity with human warmth, owned completion iconography, visible governance.** All three territories express this position through different metaphors; none of them can be mistaken for the four occupied centres.

## 11. Naming territories *(exploration space for Q10 — no recommendation yet; trademark screen required before shortlisting)*

1. **Duration-literal** ("Twelve Weeks", "Twelve") — clear, descriptive, weak trademark, *The 12 Week Year* proximity to resolve.
2. **Completion-anchored** (finishing, done-ness: e.g. words in the *Finish/Made/Through* space) — owns the brand promise directly.
3. **Journey/waypoint** (expedition vocabulary: e.g. *Waymark/Traverse* space) — pairs naturally with Territory C.
4. **Craft/instrument** (measured-time vocabulary: e.g. *Meridian/Cadence* space) — pairs with Territory B.
5. **Chapter/volume** (narrative vocabulary: e.g. *Chapter/Twelvefold* space) — pairs with Territory A.
Screening rules: UK/EU/US trademark classes 9/41/42/44 preliminary search; App Store search-collision check; pronounceability and dictation-friendliness (voice input, D-001); no name that overpromises outcomes (claims policy applies to the name itself).
