# Art Direction C — "The Long Path" (Personal Expedition)

**Status:** Territory proposal for Gate 3 — one of three. Not selected. No screens designed.

## Strategic idea

Twelve weeks as **terrain you actually cross**. The visual world of serious journey-craft — walkers' maps, route cards, contour lines, waypoints, the quiet competence of expedition planning. Not extreme-sports adrenaline: the *Ordnance-Survey-and-a-thermos* register of purposeful travel. Uniquely, this territory gives the product's defining feature — recovery — a native visual language: **a lapse is weather; a comeback is the path resuming; the terrain crossed is never uncrossed.**

## Emotional character

Steady purpose; the morning-of-a-good-walk feeling; distance-earned pride. Feels: grounded, patient, quietly adventurous, outdoorsy-without-sweat. Never: extreme, gamified-quest, Duke-of-Edinburgh-worthy.

## Visual metaphor & system

The route and the map. A continuous **drawn path** threads the whole product: twelve stages of terrain, waypoints as milestones, today as the current leg. Cartographic system language: contour-line textures (subtle, structural, never wallpaper), route-card layouts for the daily action (leg distance = today's effort, honestly stated), waypoint markers, a legend (which *is* the accessibility key — this territory makes "never colour alone" a native aesthetic: every encoding has a mapped symbol).

## Typography philosophy

Cartographic pairing: a sturdy **humanist sans** for wayfinding/UI (candidate space: Frutiger-lineage, Source Sans, National-style — *to test*) with a characterful **slab/geometric display** for stage names and numerals (candidate space: Roslindale, Produkt-style slabs); map-label conventions (letterspaced small caps for terrain names) as seasoning, strictly rationed. Numerals: legible at "route-card" sizes, tabular for data.

## Colour philosophy

Map-derived: paper-chart ground (light) / night-navigation ground (dark: deep blue-green charcoal, not black); a **route colour** as the single identity accent (the drawn-path hue — candidate space: OS-map magenta-red or trail-blaze orange); terrain neutrals (moss, stone, sand tones) as the supporting register, used structurally (stage differentiation) not decoratively. Programme accents = each programme's route colour. Semantic colours separate and conventional. Contrast discipline: chart-reading heritage is high-contrast by definition — AA is easy here, AAA reachable for core reading.

## Image & illustration direction

Cartographic illustration (the system's own terrain language) for structure; instructional illustration/video for demonstrations in a consistent field-guide style (clear line, honest bodies of all ages — D-001 representation rule: the illustrated world must visibly include older adults). No stock landscape photography (the terrain is *yours*, abstract — not Iceland).

## Motion philosophy

Travel-paced: the path draws forward (never backward); map pans glide with cartographic inertia; waypoint arrival is a settled pin, not a firework. The week-12 moment: the full route revealed from above — the journey seen whole. Reduced-motion: the path as pre-drawn states; the reveal as a designed still panorama (equal dignity).

## Iconography & data visualisation

Map-legend icons: waypoint diamonds, camp/rest markers, weather glyphs (the lapse vocabulary), single-weight and symbol-first (accessibility-native). Dataviz as elevation profiles and route progress — trend *is* terrain profile; annotated in words.

## Signature-experience fit

Strongest pairing: **The Route Line** (its definitive home) and **Evidence Waypoints**; the Accumulation Artefact renders as the cairn (a stone added per day — tactile, ancient, ownable).

## Audience & commercial fit

- **D-001 fit: excellent.** Walking/journey metaphors are the most age-universal physical vocabulary in the culture (ramblers skew *older*; route apps skew younger — both feel ownership); low-digital-confidence users read maps better than dashboards.
- **Category-neutral: excellent with one watch-item** — the metaphor is goal-agnostic (a writing journey crosses terrain too), but copy must police outdoorsy vocabulary bleeding into universal strings (paper test: the coding programme's week 5 must not mention "summits").
- **Commercial:** warm, differentiated, story-rich (marketing writes itself: "the long way is the way"); zero collision with the four occupied aesthetic centres or Sleep Cycle.

## Strengths / Risks

**Strengths:** only territory where *recovery* is aesthetically native (weather/detour vocabulary destigmatises lapse by design); richest week-12 payoff (the seen-whole route); strongest narrative for the comeback-story brand asset.
**Risks:** metaphor-management burden (terrain language creeping toward theme-park; guard: metaphor budget per surface in the design system); cartographic texture can noise up small screens (guard: contour use rules); "journey" is the wellness industry's most exhausted *word* — the differentiation is in executing the *craft* of maps, not saying journey (lexicon watch).

## Production implications

Medium-high: custom cartographic system (path engine, terrain textures, legend iconography) + illustration style guide; the path engine is real design-engineering (worth it only if this territory wins); demonstration media as per others.
