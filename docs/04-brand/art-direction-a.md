# Art Direction A — "The Printed Programme" (Editorial Transformation)

**Status:** Territory proposal for Gate 3 — one of three. Not selected. No screens designed.

## Strategic idea

Your twelve weeks as **a serious book being written about you**. The product borrows the authority and warmth of the best print — training manuals, field guides, considered magazines — because print is where humans already trust structured, expert, paced guidance. Weeks are chapters; today is the open page; the week-12 report is the finished volume on your shelf. The brand's evidence-based promise (named authors, citations) lives *natively* here: this is the territory where governance looks like beauty, not compliance.

## Emotional character

Quiet authority; the pleasure of a well-made book; being *written about* rather than tracked. Feels: considered, literate, permanent. Never: dusty, academic, beige.

## Visual metaphor & system

The page and the press. Structural grid from book design (strong margins, a real baseline grid, folios); chapter openers for weeks; running headers for orientation ("Week 3 · Building consistency"); the guided action as a beautifully set recipe/protocol page; marginalia for "why this matters" (citations as elegant footnotes, tappable). Paper-adjacent surfaces (warm near-whites / deep ink darks), letterpress-weight rules and hairlines — **no skeuomorphic paper texture**; the print feeling comes from typography and structure, not effects.

## Typography philosophy

The load-bearer. A contemporary **text serif with real italics and strong small sizes** for guidance prose (candidate space: Literata, Newsreader, Source Serif, Tiempos Text — *to be tested per typography-strategy, not chosen*), paired with a quiet grotesk for UI chrome and a **tabular-numeral** style for data positions. Editorial scale contrast (big chapter numerals, calm body). Risk to manage: serif body text at small sizes on low-DPI Android — the testing protocol's first question.

## Colour philosophy

Ink-first: near-black ink on warm paper-white; dark mode as "night printing" (warm dark surface, softened ink). One restrained identity accent used like a printer's second colour (a deep vermilion/oxblood space — candidate, not chosen) for the signature object and primary actions only. Programme accents = each programme gets its own second-colour, assigned from an accessible palette (category-neutral chrome preserved).

## Image & illustration direction

Technical-manual illustration: precise, instructive line work (exercise forms, technique steps) in the ink+accent scheme — illustration *is* the demonstration medium where video isn't warranted. Photography only as user evidence (their artefacts, their journey), never stock aspiration.

## Motion philosophy

Print-born restraint: page-turn-weight transitions (opacity/slide, no 3D curl), ink-fill progress (the week's pages filling), a settling quality — nothing bounces. Reduced-motion: crossfades + the ink-fill rendered as stepped states (designed, equal).

## Iconography & data visualisation

Icons as printer's marks: hairline, geometric, slightly literary (a folio corner, a section mark). Dataviz as book diagrams: clean axes, ink lines, annotated in words ("rising since week 4") — the editorial voice extends into charts.

## Signature-experience fit

Strongest pairing: **The Bound Volume** and **Week-Turn** (chapter close ritual); the Twelve-Segment object renders here as twelve gathered signatures/spine bands.

## Audience & commercial fit

- **D-001 fit: excellent.** Print literacy spans every adult age; serif warmth reads respectfully to 65+ without alienating 25-year-olds (the new-editorial aesthetic is currently *fashionable* with younger users — a rare both-ends win). High-legibility discipline is native to the territory.
- **Category-neutral: excellent** — chapters hold a 5K programme and a novel-writing programme with equal grace (D-004's natural home).
- **Commercial:** premium-justifying; differentiated hard against all four occupied aesthetic centres; App-Store-editorial-feature friendly.

## Strengths / Risks

**Strengths:** deepest fit with evidence-based positioning; typography-led = cheap to execute *well* relative to media-heavy directions; ages gracefully; genuinely unownable by tracker competitors (their information density can't live in this system).
**Risks:** could drift bookish-precious if the coach's directness isn't protected (mitigation: voice rules override aesthetics); serif rendering on low-end Android (test first); "editorial app" is rising as a trend — needs the signature object to stay distinct within the trend (mitigation: the Bound Volume mechanic is ours).

## Production implications

Lowest media cost of the three (illustration-forward); highest typographic discipline cost (real type QA across scales/platforms); custom illustration style guide needed before programme #1 media.
