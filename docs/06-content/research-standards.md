# Research Standards

**Status:** Framework draft for Gate 2. How evidence enters this product — the operational meaning of the founder's "based on books and researches and proven techniques". Seeded by the verified evidence base in [Annex D](../02-research/annex-d-behavioural-evidence.md) and the [source audit](../02-research/source-audit.md) discipline.

## 1. Evidence tiers (every claim maps to one)

| Tier | What | May support |
|------|------|-------------|
| E1 | Systematic reviews / meta-analyses in peer-reviewed journals | "Research shows…" statements; programme design pillars |
| E2 | Individual peer-reviewed studies (RCTs > observational) | "Research suggests…"; design choices with tier stated honestly |
| E3 | Authoritative guidance (NHS, WHO, NICE-type bodies; recognised professional-body position stands) | Safety boundaries, healthy-default guidance |
| E4 | Expert consensus / practitioner-standard textbooks | Technique instruction ("standard practice is…") |
| E5 | Reputable popular works (the "books" of the founder's brief) | Framing and narrative only — **never** outcome claims; cited as inspiration, not evidence |
| E0 | Vendor data, blogs, single anecdotes | Nothing user-facing; internal context only |

Rules: outcome-adjacent statements require E1–E3; mechanism explanations ("why this matters") E1–E4 with the tier's honest verb; E5 named as what it is ("popularised by…"). The Annex D convention (verified vs established-not-reverified) carries into every citation pack.

## 2. Citation standards

Each programme ships an **evidence pack**: full citations (authors, year, journal/publisher, DOI/URL), what each source actually establishes (one sentence — the source-audit discipline), which programme statements it supports, tier, and access date. In-app: "why this matters" panels cite by name+year with a tap-through to the pack's plain-language entry (FR-80). No citation laundering: a secondary source citing a primary does not become the primary.

## 3. Interpretation discipline *(the founder's Part-3 correction list, made permanent)*

Banned moves, checked at review: overgeneralising a narrow dataset (state the studied population); correlation dressed as causation ("linked to" ≠ "causes"); stale evidence presented as current (packs carry dates; contradicting newer evidence triggers re-review); inference presented as fact (label professional inference as such); popularity presented as proof (E5 discipline); effect sizes hidden behind "proven" (state magnitude honestly where users' expectations depend on it — "a moderate effect in studies" beats "transforms").

## 4. The standing evidence base (platform-level, already verified)

Programme-independent design claims this product may make, with their anchors (full detail Annex D): habit formation timelines (Lally 2010; Singh et al. 2024); goal specificity (Locke & Latham 2002); implementation intentions (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, d=0.65); progress monitoring (Harkin et al. 2016, d=0.40); fresh-start effect (Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014); streak/lapse dynamics (Silverman & Barasch 2023; Marlatt & Gordon 1985; Polivy & Herman lineage); reflection (Anseel 2009; Di Stefano working paper — cited as such); attrition reality (Eysenbach 2005; Meyerowitz-Katz 2020; Baumel 2019 with scope). The 12-week frame is described as "a well-precedented window", never "scientifically optimal" (Annex D §8 rule).

## 5. Evidence watch

Quarterly: spot-check packs of live programmes against new publications (author responsibility, reviewer sign-off at annual review); the correction-log mechanism applies to content exactly as to strategy docs — corrections are transparent, dated, and user-messaged when material (governance §6).
