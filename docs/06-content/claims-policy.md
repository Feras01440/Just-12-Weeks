# Claims Policy

**Status:** Framework draft for Gate 2. Governs every claim, everywhere: in-app copy, programme content, store listings, screenshots, ads, press, share cards. One policy — a claim too strong for the App Store is too strong for a tweet.

## 1. The prime rules

1. **No outcome guarantees.** Never "you will lose X", "whiter teeth in 12 weeks", "guaranteed results", "everyone can". The promise is structure, guidance and honest evidence of the user's own change — not predetermined outcomes.
2. **Evidence verbs match evidence tiers** (research-standards §1): E1 "research shows"; E2 "research suggests / in studies"; E3 "health guidance recommends"; E4 "standard practice is"; E5 "popularised by". "Proven" is banned outright (the founder brief's word — implemented as this policy, which is what makes it *true*).
3. **The user's results are the user's.** Real testimonials/case evidence (post-launch, consented) presented with context, never as typical-outcome implications; no fabricated testimonials, statistics, or social proof, ever (founder requirement; also law).
4. **Nothing medical.** No diagnosis, treatment, prevention, or cure language for any condition; no "boosts immunity / fixes / heals"; medical-adjacent topics carry authored signposting (governance §3). Weight-related copy follows eating-disorder-aware rules: no crash promises, no daily-weight emphasis, no body-shame framing, no before/after imagery in *marketing* (in-app private evidence is the user's own business — D-003).
5. **Honest numbers only.** Any figure in public copy must pass the source-audit bar (primary-verified) — internal planning numbers (†-flagged) never appear externally.
6. **Time/cost honesty.** Stated session lengths, weekly time, and prices are accurate including the boring cases (renewal price adjacent to any intro price — paywall principles).

## 2. The six-line boundary in copy terms

Education/guidance/wellness language is ours; anything reading as individualised prescription, diagnosis, or treatment is rewritten or cut (governance §3). Test: *"Could a reasonable reader believe this app assessed **their** condition or promised **their** outcome?"* If yes, it fails.

## 3. Surface-specific rules

- **Store listings & screenshots:** claims-policy compliant end to end; screenshots show real product states (no mocked data implying outcomes); subtitle/keywords avoid outcome-promise phrasing; no category-leading superlatives ("the #1…") without a citable basis.
- **Paywall:** what's included stated concretely; benefit language about the *product* (guidance, programmes, recovery), not the user's guaranteed transformation.
- **Notifications:** no claim-bearing copy at all (they direct, they don't sell).
- **Share cards / completion reports:** state what the evidence shows ("12 weeks · 57 guided sessions · first 5K completed") — facts of the journey, not extrapolated promises; the report's honest-close section (week-12 spec §2.2.5) is claims-policy's voice inside the product.
- **Ads/press (future):** every asset passes this policy + the ASA's basic tests (legal, decent, honest, truthful) before spend.

## 4. Banned lexicon (claims positions)

guaranteed · proven (bare) · transform your life · melt/blast/shred/torch · miracle · effortless · scientifically proven (bare) · doctor-approved (unless a named doctor approved that exact content in governance) · clinically shown (unless the cited trial studied *this* programme — it didn't) · "results in N days" · anything the pressure lexicon (content strategy §3) already bans.

## 5. Approval flow

Every claim-bearing surface passes: author → copy-edit (lexicons) → claims check against the evidence pack → (class-2 content) reviewer sign-off → publish. Marketing surfaces additionally get founder sign-off. Violations found post-publish follow governance §6 correction mechanics — including public correction where the claim was public.
