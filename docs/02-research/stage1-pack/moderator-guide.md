# Interview Moderator Guide

**Status:** Execution-ready. This supersedes and absorbs the script outline that lived in `user-research-plan.md` §5 (that section now points here — single source, no drift). 45 minutes, five sections + close. The discipline: **past behaviour before opinions, concept exposure last, silence is a tool.**

## Moderation technique (read before every session until it's reflex)

- Ask, then **count to five** in your head. The second answer after a silence is usually the true one.
- Follow the story, not the script order — but bank every section's core questions before the clock beats you.
- Probe with their words: "you said it 'just faded' — what did a faded week actually look like?"
- Never rescue them into positivity ("but you did well to try!") — sympathy corrupts data; warmth without verdicts.
- Never explain or defend the concept, even in section E. You are not selling; you are weighing.
- Mark verbatims live with quote marks in notes; everything else is paraphrase by default.
- If they ask "is this for an app?": "I'll tell you everything at the end — first I want your experience untainted by my idea."

## Timings at a glance

A context 5' · B the last attempt 13' · C money & the shape of help 10' · D the 12-week frame 7' · E concept & recovery exposure 8' · close 2'.

## A — Context (5 min)

1. "Tell me a bit about a typical week for you right now."
2. "When you think about something you've been wanting to change or get better at — what comes to mind first?"
*(Purpose: rapport + their language for goals. Note the words they use; you'll reuse them.)*

## B — The last attempt (13 min; the core)

3. "Tell me about the last time you seriously tried to [their S2 goal]. Start from the day you decided."
4. "What did you actually do in the first week?"
5. "Walk me through when it started to slip. What was happening that week?" *(Probe for the specific day/moment, not the theory.)*
6. "What did you tell yourself at that point?" *(Verbatim gold — capture exactly.)*
7. "Did anything or anyone almost pull you back? What happened?"
8. "What happened to whatever you were using — the app, plan, notebook, membership?"
*(Watch for: the invisible-results gap, the all-or-nothing collapse, the shame spiral, the restart pattern. Do not name any of these to them.)*

## C — Money and the shape of help (10 min)

9. "What have you spent money on for this goal — apps, memberships, products, courses? Roughly how much, all in?" *(WTP evidence: real past spend, per concept-and-landing-tests §5.)*
10. "Which of those felt worth it? Which felt like a waste? Why?"
11. "If you've used any apps for this: what made you keep or delete them?" *(Listen for paywall timing, notification fatigue, streak anxiety — never prompt these words.)*
11a. "When you were in the middle of it, what kind of help did you actually want on a given day — someone telling you exactly what to do, showing you how, keeping you company, or keeping score?" *(The guidance-vs-tracking probe — the single most load-bearing question in the study.)*
11b. "Was there a moment you didn't know *how* to do the thing properly? What did you do?"

## D — The 12-week frame (7 min; still no pitch)

12. "Some people commit to a fixed period — say, twelve weeks — for a goal like this. Have you ever done something with a fixed length? How did it go?"
13. "Imagine you're four weeks into a twelve-week commitment and you miss five days. What do you honestly do next?" *(Their answer is the recovery baseline for E2.)*
14. "How do you know, today, whether you're actually making progress on [goal]? What do you look at?"

## E — Exposure (8 min; concept last, always)

**E1. Concept statements.** "I'm going to read you three short descriptions of a possible product — there's no right answer and I didn't write them to be liked. After each: what do you think it *is*, and what would you expect it to do?" Read the three statements from [concept-and-landing-tests §1] **in rotated order** (rotate per participant; log the order). Then: "Which one, if any, sounds like it was written for someone like you? Why that one?"
**E2. Recovery-flow concept test (script).** "One more scenario. You're in week five of a twelve-week programme in this app. Life went sideways and you missed four days. You open the app. I'll read you what it says:"
> *"Four days away, week five. This is the most common moment in any twelve weeks — the plan is built for it. Everything you completed still stands. You've got three ways forward: pick up today with a shorter session; reshape this week to fit reality; or pause properly and choose your return day."*
Then, in order: (a) "What just happened there, in your own words?" (b) "How does that compare with what apps you've used actually do?" (c) "Which of the three doors would *you* take, honestly?" (d) "Is there anything about that message you don't believe or don't like?" *(Capture word choices — the card-sort words "understanding/practical/guilt-tripping/naggy" get checked at synthesis, never offered aloud.)*
**E3. First-week proof.** 15. "What would it have to prove to you in the first week for you to keep it on your phone?" 16. "What would make it feel like every other habit app you've deleted?"

## Close (2 min)

"Genuinely useful — thank you. Two last things: who's *one person* you know who's attempted something like this in the past year and might talk to me? And the voucher — I'll send it today." Send it today.

## Banned moves (the leading-question lexicon)

Never ask: "Would you use/pay for this?" · "Do you think 12 weeks is a good length?" · "Would you like [feature]?" · "Don't you find streaks stressful?" — and never supply the words *guidance, tracker, streak, recovery, accountability* before the participant does. Every deviation gets logged in the session note's method field.
