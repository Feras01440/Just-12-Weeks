# Synthetic Stress Test

**Status:** SYNTHETIC MATERIAL — clearly labelled hypothesis generation, 26 July 2026. **This is not user research and must never be cited as evidence of user behaviour** (R-19 guards this). Purpose: stress the product thesis from constructed perspectives so the passive validation system and any escalated interviews test the *right* questions. Every finding here remains an **Unvalidated hypothesis** until genuine behaviour supports or refutes it. Method: a 14-profile panel spanning the D-001 age bands, digital confidence, consistency history, accessibility needs, finances, goal archetypes and attitude extremes; three perspectives run with maker–checker separation — the sceptical-customer and adversarial-critic passes were produced by an **independent reviewer** who had no hand in the product documents; the supportive-advocate pass and this consolidation were produced separately. Outputs feed: [objection-register.md](objection-register.md) and [assumption-failure-scenarios.md](assumption-failure-scenarios.md).

## Perspective 1 — Supportive product advocate (SYNTHETIC — hypothesis only)

The advocate's honest best case, run against the same roster (ids reference §1 below):

- **The guidance thesis lands hardest on P04, P05, P06 and P12** — the logging-averse parent gets a product that finally asks nothing; the structure-hungry get told what to do by something that adapts; the app-anxious get a paywall that says the renewal date out loud; the 74-year-old gets pacing that respects him. These four are the advocate's core market and all four map to Pattern A.
- **Recovery is the first mechanic this roster has never seen.** Even sceptics (P01) have no reference class for an app that plans the comeback; if week-5 reality matches the promise once, word-of-mouth writes itself ("it didn't punish me").
- **The 12-week container converts intention to commitment for P10, P08, P13** — finishers and deadline-holders want edges; open-ended apps never gave them one.
- **The honesty stack compounds:** named reviewers (P08's demand), renewal reminders (P01's disqualifier answered), suitability truth (P09's safety need) — each is niche alone; together they form a trust profile no incumbent in the 31-product audit holds.
- **Age-inclusivity is an unoccupied position** (no competitor documents accessibility at all — verified gap G6): P09/P10/P11/P12 are unserved, reachable, and loyal when served.
- **The advocate's honest concessions:** the case is weakest for P02 (free alternatives) and P03/P14 (autonomy/irony cohorts) — the advocate would *not* target them at launch; and the advocate agrees with the critic that week-1-free under-demonstrates the differentiators (the advocate's fix: make the *paywall itself* narrate recovery and completion, showing what can't be experienced yet).

## §2 Sceptic pass — SYNTHETIC — hypothesis only

**P01 — Maya, 24, ex-subscriber of five apps**
- First reaction: "'Guided 12-week transformation' — so a fitness app that found a classier font. I've heard 'we're different' from every app I've cancelled."
- Bounce: the paywall, whatever it says — the moment she sees "renews automatically" she's gone; the word "automatically" is disqualifying regardless of the reminder promise.
- Won't believe: *"We'll remind you before each renewal."* — "Every app says something like that. The reminder always arrives after the charge or never."
- Win-back: a non-renewing one-off purchase option visible on the same screen, and proof (screenshot culture) of an actual reminder arriving.

**P02 — Josh, 19, learning Python**
- First reaction: "freeCodeCamp is free and made by actual developers. What does £20 buy me — a schedule?"
- Bounce: day 2, when the 'guided action' says practise on a laptop and he realises the app is a phone wrapper around work that happens elsewhere.
- Won't believe: *"Tells you exactly what to do today and shows you how."* — "It can't show me how to debug *my* code. It's going to show me a generic exercise."
- Win-back: a visible syllabus better than his bookmarked roadmap, and a term price under what he spends on coffee in a fortnight.

**P03 — Elena, 28, novelist**
- First reaction: "An app is going to *tell* me what to write today? That's not how creative work functions."
- Bounce: browsing the catalogue and finding no writing programme at launch — "so it's a fitness/coding app cosplaying as a life platform" — or, if one exists, the first prescriptive daily instruction.
- Won't believe: *"The app understands my goal."* — "It stored a sentence I typed. That is not understanding."
- Win-back: evidence a named, published writer authored the programme, and structure framed as scaffolding she controls, not direction she follows.

**P04 — Priya, 38, logging-averse parent**
- First reaction: "Minimal input, no forms — good, but every app said that and then asked me to weigh my breakfast."
- Bounce: first contextual micro-question after a 5:40am toddler wake — one question too many *is* the form; also the recovery screen knowing "four days away" — "so it *was* counting."
- Won't believe: *"No daily forms, ever."* — "Week 1, maybe. Week 5 there'll be a survey."
- Win-back: a genuine two-tap day demonstrated on day one, and lapse copy that never quotes the number of days she missed back at her.

**P05 — Marcus, 41, wants a drill sergeant**
- First reaction: "Warm and understanding is what I do to myself already. I pay people to not accept my excuses."
- Bounce: the first time a missed day is met with "life happened" — "I didn't have a life event, I was lazy, and it should say so"; the gentler-option offer reads as the app not believing in him.
- Won't believe: *"Recovery is designed, never punished."* — "Accountability without consequence isn't accountability."
- Win-back: a firm-tone setting ("hold me to it"), and adaptation that raises the bar when he's cruising, not only lowers it when he slips.

**P06 — Carmen, 45, app-anxious**
- First reaction: interested in the promise, frightened of the machinery — "these things take your card and you can never make it stop."
- Bounce: the free-text "goal in your own words" box (freezes — is there a wrong answer?), or the sentence "cancel in your App Store subscriptions" — she doesn't know where that is.
- Won't believe: *"Cancel anytime — takes about a minute."* — "For you, maybe."
- Win-back: an in-app, plain-words cancel walkthrough with pictures, and a person (her daughter) confirming she wasn't charged after cancelling.

**P07 — Dan, 31, night-shift nurse**
- First reaction: "'What to do today' assumes I have a today. My Tuesday is your Wednesday night."
- Bounce: the recovery conversation appearing after a normal run of nights — being 'recovered' for living his rota; that mis-read once and the app is deleted.
- Won't believe: *"Adapts when real life interrupts the plan."* — "Every app's 'adapt' means it moves a reminder."
- Win-back: shift-pattern scheduling at setup (weeks defined by his rota, not the calendar) and lapse detection that knows a planned gap from a real one.

**P08 — Robert, 56, guitarist, a finisher**
- First reaction: "Twelve weeks of guitar from the company that also does deadlifts and Python. Specialists exist for a reason."
- Bounce: opening the catalogue and seeing four unrelated domains with the same visual template — reads as content-mill breadth, not depth; or hearing plain-language copy pitched at "reading age 9–11" — "I'm not nine."
- Won't believe: *"Professionally authored, evidence-based, with named expert review"* — in *every* category. "Who is this omniscient faculty?"
- Win-back: one named, verifiable domain authority per programme (a guitarist he could look up), and depth in his category even at the cost of catalogue breadth.

**P09 — Lin, 58, low vision, post-health-scare**
- First reaction: "Promising — but if 'how to do it correctly' is a video of someone's silhouette, I can't use it."
- Bounce: the first demonstration that exists only as video with no described alternative; or a suitability screen that collects her health conditions before she trusts the company holding them.
- Won't believe: *"For adults of every age, across digital confidence levels."* — "Every app claims that; then the text doesn't scale and the buttons are grey on grey."
- Win-back: real 200% text on screen one, audio-described or text-equivalent demonstrations, and a plain statement of who sees her health answers.

**P10 — Frank, 62, tremor, ex-forces**
- First reaction: "A plan with a start and an end — finally. But the tone had better talk to me like an adult who's run harder programmes than this."
- Bounce: mistapping the 'easier version' with his tremor and being unable to cleanly undo; or the first "that's part of twelve weeks" line — "don't manage my feelings, give me the order."
- Won't believe: *"£19.99 every 12 weeks"* as a budgetable number — "Every 12 weeks lands on a different day each quarter. My budget is monthly."
- Win-back: large stable targets with confirm-before-commit, an undo everywhere, and a fixed, predictable payment date.

**P11 — Grace, 67, screen-reader user**
- First reaction: "I'll know within one minute whether they mean it. VoiceOver on, first screen."
- Bounce: one unlabelled button in the guided-action player or a 'signature card' that reads as "image, button, button" — instant uninstall; she's done twelve accessibility rodeos.
- Won't believe: *"Premium in craft."* — "Premium visual craft usually means custom controls, and custom controls usually mean broken accessibility."
- Win-back: the recovery conversation, weekly review and paywall all fully navigable by screen reader — and an accessibility statement that names what was tested, on record.

**P12 — Arthur, 74, fixed pension**
- First reaction: "It sounds kind. But is it *for* me, or will it assume I can get on the floor and back up?"
- Bounce: the moment payment appears — "automatic renewal" to a man who once fought a gym direct debit for three months is a hard stop; or onboarding that turns him away ("recommend against starting") with nowhere warm to land.
- Won't believe: *"Finish with real evidence of change."* — "At my age I'll believe change when my balance is better, not when an app writes me a report."
- Win-back: his GP or physio recognising the programme's reviewer, a non-renewing payment his son can set up once, and week 1 free so he can see it respects his pace.

**P13 — Nicole, 48, wedding in 14 weeks**
- First reaction: "Twelve weeks is exactly my window. But 'honest about what's achievable' sounds like an app preparing to lower my target."
- Bounce: the suitability/expectation step telling her the dress-size goal isn't healthily reachable in the window — right message, but she came for the goal, not the truth; she'll go find an app that lies.
- Won't believe: *"Progress visible before results are."* — "Capability language doesn't fit into the dress. Either the zip closes or it doesn't."
- Win-back: an honest reframe that still gives her a concrete wedding-day outcome she can want ("here's what twelve good weeks actually gets you, in numbers"), not just a warning.

**P14 — Theo, 26, self-help-allergic**
- First reaction: "'Meaningful goal.' 'Journey.' 'Transformation.' This is LinkedIn-core with a calm font."
- Bounce: the first coach-voice sentence — "life happened, this week is a clean page" reads as scripted-therapist copy he'll screenshot and mock; also nothing to show anyone until week 12 — "so the app has no personality and no proof."
- Won't believe: *"No dark patterns."* — "The absence of countdown timers doesn't mean the kindness isn't a funnel. The 'clean page' still points at the renew button."
- Win-back: dry, unsentimental copy that never says "journey", and one moment of week-1 competence so surprising he screenshots it for the group chat unironically.

## §3 Adversarial pass — SYNTHETIC — hypothesis only

### 3.1 Misunderstood promises
- **"Transformation" is heard as outcome, sold as process.** The PRD promise ("guided, measurable 12-week transformation") plus the claims policy ("never guaranteed outcomes") means the marketing noun and the legal noun are different things. P13 buys the outcome, receives a process, and files the gap as deception at week 8. Refund/1-star vector.
- **"Guided" is heard as responsive.** "The app understands my goal / adjusts when I can't" (experience-principles governing quote) implies a listener. It is authored content plus rules. The promise breaks the first time a user asks something off-script (P05's form question, P09's "is this safe with my medication", P07's rota). No AI content is a stated non-goal, so the gap is structural, not a maturity issue.
- **"Recovery designed" is heard as "skipping is fine"** by users who want edge (P05, P10) — compassion parsed as low expectations — and as **"it's watching me"** by logging-averse users (P04) when the flow recites "four days away, week 5."
- **"One goal at a time" is heard as artificial restriction** (P03, P14): adults run parallel goals; a hard one-goal frame reads as either dogma or an upsell architecture for sequential purchases.
- **"12 weeks" is heard as the fitness-genre unit.** "12 week shred/challenge" owns that phrase in search and culture. The category-neutral platform inherits a category-loaded name-frame (attacks R-11 work before it starts).

### 3.2 Weak assumptions
- **The price anchor is asserted, not earned** (thesis "Why users pay"). Users anchor to the *surface* they buy on: an app store, where the reference class is £0–£9.99. "You'd pay a PT £50/session" transfers only if the product *feels* like a person; it feels like content. P01/P02/P06/P12 all anchor low.
- **The Restarter paradox**: Pattern A is defined by quitting and by a "history of lapsed memberships" — then asked to pre-commit money to the longest billing unit in the app family. The segment's defining trait is the model's conversion killer ("I'll waste this like the gym membership"). Also the docs' own citations (3–4% 30-day retention, 66-day automaticity) predict this cohort's behaviour *inside this app too*.
- **Recovery ≥35% metric measures survivors, not saves** (recovery-experience §6): `recovery_flow_entered → returned` only counts lapsers who *opened the app*. The notification policy (3 messages then principled silence) caps reach. The true denominator — all lapsers — is never in the metric. The differentiator can read green while the thesis fails.
- **"Progress visible before results" underdelivers exactly where results-hunger is highest.** Capability checkpoints work for guitar and Python; for appearance/weight goals (P13, P01) the honest weeks-2–6 evidence is "you completed things" — which is what a tracker shows. D-003's menu is thinnest for the most commercial segment.
- **Three difficulty variants ≈ personalisation** is asserted; a rota nurse, a tremor, a toddler and a wedding are not three levels of one plan.

### 3.3 Age-related friction
- **Cancellation is outsourced to the stores** (paywall-principles §1.4): "cancel in your App Store subscriptions — takes about a minute" is true for P01 and fictional for P06/P12. The honest *sentence* does not create the *capability*; auto-renew fear alone is a non-start for 65+ tight-finance profiles.
- **Media-heavy "show me how" fails accessibility at the core, not the edges**: video demonstrations without described/text equivalents exclude P09/P11 from the product's central verb. The signature card system + sheets/peeks (today-experience §2) is exactly the custom-control territory where screen readers historically break (P11's minute-one test).
- **Tremor (P10)**: one-tap-hero design is fine; variant pickers, sheet dismissal, and any tap-target near another are not. "Every gesture has a visible alternative" needs an "every tap has an undo" sibling; docs don't state one.
- **Reading-age 9–11 vs "no condescension"**: the floor is specified and tested; the *ceiling* (adults feeling talked down to — P08, P10) has no acceptance test anywhere in G1–G10.
- **Young-end friction is real too**: stripping streaks, gamification, social and shareables removes every mechanic P01/P14's cohort actually responds to, with nothing screenshot-worthy until week 12. Calm and boring are indistinguishable in a 20-second store evaluation.

### 3.4 Trust objections
- **Category contamination**: subscription self-help/fitness apps poisoned the well; "no dark patterns" is a claim every dark-pattern app also makes. Unverifiable-at-purchase promises ("we'll remind you") price at zero for P01.
- **"Named review" without famous names is decoration**: users can't evaluate credentials (P08); an unknown reviewer's name adds compliance, not trust. Trust would come from *verifiable* domain authorities — expensive, contradicting the content-cost failure mode the thesis already admits.
- **Twelve weeks is a long bet on a small company**: pre-paying a term to an unknown brand raises "will this exist in week 9, and where does my health data go if it doesn't" (P06, P08, P11, P09). The suitability screen collects sensitive answers *before* trust exists.
- **Catalogue-vs-platform bait risk**: marketing says "any meaningful goal"; launch ships 1–2 programmes (Q1/Q3). P03 arrives for writing, finds fitness and coding, and files the platform claim as bait.

### 3.5 Onboarding risks
- **The honest turn-away lands at peak intent** (FR-11 "may recommend against starting"): P09/P12/P13 — the highest-need, highest-conversion moments — can be screened out or frightened by suitability honesty with no designed warm landing. Right principle; unmeasured cost; no metric owns it.
- **Question arithmetic**: ≤5 personalisation questions *plus* up-to-6 governance suitability questions *plus* 18+ *plus* account = the "no interrogation" brand can still deliver a dozen decisions before day 1 for the anxious (P06).
- **The free-text goal field is a trap both ways**: P06 stalls on it; P03/P14 write something specific and then notice the guidance never references their words — quietly falsifying "the app understands my goal" on day 2.
- **Start-date choice invites deferral**: "today / Monday / custom" hands the classic procrastination ramp to the exact segment defined by deferral (Pattern A).
- **Week-1-free previews the most generic slice**: the differentiators (recovery, adaptation, week-12 completion) are all structurally undemonstrable in seven good days. The trial shows a calm checklist; the paywall then asks premium money for qualities the user has to take on faith (P01, P02, P14). The thesis names "paywall-before-value" as a failure mode; the sharper point is that *this* product's value is back-loaded by design, so no compliant preview can show it.

### 3.6 Guidance risks — where "guidance" reads wrong, per profile
- **Controlling**: P03 (creative) — daily prescription of creative work reads as the app colonising the one domain where autonomy *is* the point. P14 — being "guided" into a morning routine by an app is the thing he mocks.
- **Rigid**: P07 — "one clear action today" presumes day-shaped lives; weeks-as-calendar breaks on rotas; the only reshape tool lives in *recovery*, i.e. the app treats his normal life as a lapse.
- **Patronising**: P10/P08 — warm-coach + plain language + "offered without judgement" reads as therapeutic hand-holding to people whose identity is discipline. P05 inverts it: the always-available easier option reads as the app pre-forgiving him — "it expects me to fail."
- **Underpowered**: the moment guidance can't answer a real question (form check, error message, chord buzzing), "tell-show-guide-adapt" collapses to "play video, mark done" — and the user re-categorises the product as content-with-a-checklist (P02, P05, P09).
- **One voice cannot serve this roster**: the tone spec ("warm, specific, never drill-sergeant") is a *position*, not a universal. It structurally cannot satisfy P05 (wants drill), P14 (wants dry), and P12 (wants reassurance) simultaneously; docs treat tone as solved rather than segmented.

### 3.7 Recovery-flow failure cases
- **The flow can't reach the person it's for**: most lapsers never reopen the app; three quiet notifications then silence is principled and low-reach. The celebrated conversation plays to an empty room (P04, P01). The abstinence-violation insight cuts both ways: the shame that kills return happens *outside* the app, before any copy can help.
- **Surveillance backfire**: "Four days away, week 5. This is the most common moment…" — specificity proves counting. For P04 (logging-averse) and P14 (irony), being *seen* is the trigger, not the comfort. Guilt-by-kindness: warmth that itemises your absence.
- **Groundhog-Day recovery**: no designed n-th-lapse variant. The third identical "clean page" conversation reads as script, and the user hears the app forgiving them on a loop — which either loses meaning (P01) or manufactures obligation (P06 feels she's "letting the nice app down": guilt smuggled in through kindness, the exact dynamic the blame-lexicon was built to prevent).
- **Lightened re-entry as demotion**: despite "variant dignity rules", P05/P10 read the lightened week as the app lowering the bar; P13 reads *any* reshaping as theft of her fixed deadline — there is no "compress/intensify" door, only lighten/reflow/pause, so the deadline user's honest option set is "accept less or leave."
- **Pause as flattering churn**: a dignified, pressure-free pause is also a socially costless exit. Expect a graveyard of eternal pauses that never count as churn — metrics comfort, business rot.
- **Recovery × entitlement collision**: lapse across a term boundary and the comeback moment meets the re-lock face (F18) or a renewal charge instead of the fresh-start conversation. Paywall-principles bans paywalls *inside* recovery flows, but expiry doesn't consult lapse state; the two proudest designs collide at the most fragile moment (P04, P12).
- **Misdetection admitted but fatal**: docs (§7) note scheduled-gap misfires; one recovery conversation after P07's normal night block, or P10's planned rest, converts "it understands me" into "it doesn't know me" — unrecoverable for exactly the users primed to distrust apps.

### 3.8 Subscription objections — 12-week-term specifics
- **The store can't bill in 12-week units (hypothesis to verify)**: Apple/Google auto-renew periods are weekly/monthly/quarterly-shaped (1w, 1m, 2m, 3m, 6m, 1y and Play equivalents); "every 12 weeks" would in practice ship as a 3-month (~13-week) subscription *labelled* 12 weeks — a factual honesty crack in the honesty brand, and a copy/legal headache under the very DMCC regime the docs cite. Earliest evidence: attempt to configure the SKU. (P01 will find the mismatch; P14 will screenshot it.)
- **Nobody budgets in 12-week units**: charges drift through the calendar (4.33 "quarters"/year); P10/P12 on monthly budgets can't slot a drifting £19.99; P06 can't predict the date, which *is* the fear.
- **£19.99 parses three wrong ways**: as monthly (overestimate → bounce), as one-off (renewal → betrayal despite reminders — reminders get missed by exactly P12), or as "per programme? per goal? what if I switch goals mid-term?" — term-to-programme mapping is undefined in the user's head.
- **Pause vs term incoherence**: recovery offers "pause properly"; billing has no pause. A dignified 3-week pause inside a paid term means paying for 12, receiving 9 — the product's kindest feature quietly costs money, and no doc reconciles it (P04, P07). Same shape: finish in 14 real-world weeks → second term charged for a fortnight of remainder — a lapse fine wearing honest clothes.
- **M6 hybrid = decision paralysis at the worst moment**: "buy this programme once OR subscribe per term" requires the user to predict their own future multi-cycle behaviour — the one thing Restarters are worst at. Two clean offers is still a comparison problem P01 has been burned by before.
- **Lump-sum wall for the tight-finance half of the roster**: £19.99 in one hit is harder than £7.99 drip for P02/P12/P14 even when arithmetically better — the term model taxes exactly the cash-flow-constrained.
- **Refund cliff**: quit at week 3 of a paid term; store norms give no pro-rata; "honest brand keeps my money for 9 unused weeks" writes its own one-star review.

### 3.9 Category conflicts
- **Breadth is a credibility liability, not an asset, at small scale**: each vertical has a beloved specialist (P02 names freeCodeCamp, P08 names his teacher, P01 names three fitness apps). "Expert-reviewed in four domains" from one small brand pattern-matches to content mill. The platform story (D-004) is a *founder* conviction with no user pull demonstrated anywhere in the docs.
- **Skill/creative archetypes reduce the app to the thing it swears it isn't**: coding happens on a laptop; novels happen in a manuscript; guitar happens on a guitar. For those archetypes the in-app "guided action" is a briefing plus a Done button — i.e. **a tracker with prose** (attacks D-002's universality and PRQ-02's paper test). The guidance-first thesis is really a *physical/wellbeing* thesis that generalises worse than claimed.
- **Category neutrality manufactures genericness**: PRQ-01 bans category language on every universal surface, so universal copy must say "your guided action", "your meaningful goal" — abstract, corporate, exactly the generic voice R-11 fears. The neutrality mandate and the anti-generic mandate pull in opposite directions and no doc arbitrates.
- **Distribution forces a category anyway**: app stores and ASO demand a shelf; "12 weeks" search intent is fitness; acquisition economics will quietly make it a fitness app whatever the vision says. D-004 dies by keyword, not by decision.

### 3.10 Why it may still be perceived as generic
- The visible week-1 surface — card, button, calm copy — is indistinguishable in screenshots from a hundred minimal habit apps; every differentiator (recovery, adaptation, completion report, governance) is invisible at evaluation time and mostly post-paywall (P01, P14, P02).
- "Evidence-based", "no guilt", "premium", "science-backed calm" are the *current marketing dialect of the entire category* — the anti-hype position is itself crowded.
- The product's discipline (nothing else on Today, no numbers, no theatre) removes the surfaces where personality usually lives; without the Stage-3 signature system landing perfectly, restraint reads as emptiness.

### 3.11 Harm & manipulation potential — despite intentions
- **Kindness as choice architecture**: fresh-start framing, "clean page", warm re-entry — the same behavioural literature streak apps mined, aimed at resumption of a *paid* product. Three recovery notifications are re-engagement by another name; "no dark patterns" survives only while nobody maps the funnel (P14's accusation). The trust built by the renewal reminder is itself conversion lubricant.
- **Guilt-by-kindness / parasocial debt**: consistently gracious copy can create obligation ("the app has been so understanding") — guilt relocated, not removed; most potent in P06/P12, the anxious and the lonely.
- **Dependency by design**: week-13 hands the user their next goal on a platform whose revenue *requires* successive journeys; the structural incentive is a user who is never finished. "Honest renewals" and "never graduates" can be the same behaviour viewed from two sides.
- **Over-restriction**: one-goal dogma may cause users to drop healthy parallel activities to fit the frame; "the app said one thing at a time" becomes an externalised excuse to narrow life (P03).
- **Masking harm**: suitability screening is self-reported; P13-type deadline users can run crash behaviour *underneath* a legitimate-looking programme, now with an evidence-generating app that makes the fortnight look structured. The docs know clinical prep needs clinical governance; marketing gravity ("wedding", "event", "12 weeks") pulls exactly toward the risky cohort.
- **Data gravity**: goal + suitability answers + lapse patterns + weekly reflections = an intimate behavioural dataset held by a small company; retention/deletion promises are only as durable as the company (P09, P11).

## §5 Assumption-failure scenarios — SYNTHETIC — hypothesis only

**S1 — The anchor snaps.** Stage-2 pricing runs. At £24.99/12-weeks, paywall-view→purchase collapses below 1%; interviews return "that's steep *for an app*" from the same people who pay £45/month gyms. The PT-anchor never transferred because the buying moment happens on a phone next to £0 alternatives. Revenue model quietly re-plans around £9.99 — which cannot fund governance-reviewed content, reactivating the thesis's own content-cost death spiral. *Detection signal:* price-variant conversion curves plus interview verbatims — specifically the reference class users name unprompted ("app" vs "coach/course") when asked what the price compares to.

**S2 — The comeback plays to an empty room.** Six months in: the recovery metric is green (38% of flow-entrants return) and celebrated. A denominator audit shows 74% of 2–6-day lapsers never reopened the app at all; the three-then-silence notification policy reached them twice, unopened. True save rate ~10%; week-4 survival is statistically indistinguishable from a well-made tracker's. The differentiator exists mainly in decks. *Detection signal:* lapse-cohort funnel instrumented from *lapse start*, not flow entry — % reopening within 14 days vs matched habit-app baseline; recovery-notification open rates.

**S3 — Week one is the product.** M6-lite launches. Day-6 exit responses cluster on "clean, calm habit app"; zero mentions of recovery or adaptation (nobody lapsed in 6 days — by design nothing differentiating fired). Trial→paid lands at 1.8%, dominated by P05-types who wanted any structure. The team responds by adding week-1 "wow" moments — charts, celebrations — eroding the calm principles one release at a time. *Detection signal:* day-6 "what is this app / what would you tell a friend it does?" free-text; feature-request and A/B backlog drifting toward engagement mechanics (the tripwire list in today-experience §4 starts getting exception requests).

**S4 — The platform becomes a fitness app by keyword.** Launch ships fitness + Python programmes. Paid acquisition only converts on fitness terms; ASO forces "workout, 12 week plan" metadata; coding-cohort week-2 retention runs 30 points below fitness (real practice lives on desktop); store reviews say "great coach app, ignore the coding thing". Within two quarters the roadmap, brand imagery and Q1 emphasis are fitness; D-004 remains true only in the decision log. *Detection signal:* install-source keyword mix; per-archetype D7/D14 retention gap; review text-mining for category nouns; % of universal-surface copy exceptions requested by marketing.

**S5 — The kindness becomes the joke.** A mid-sized creator posts a teardown: screenshots of the recovery script ("life happened — this week is a clean page") beside the renewal screen, captioned "the app that forgives you into renewing." The warm copy — identical on the 1st and 4th lapse — reads as scripted; the day-count line reads as surveillance; the anxious cohort separately reports feeling *worse* about letting the "nice app" down. "No dark patterns" becomes the debated claim rather than the differentiator. *Detection signal:* Stage-5 simulated-lapse word-sorts split by cohort (any cohort >25% choosing "scripted/patronising/watching me"); repeat-lapse flow completion declining by lapse number; social-listening for recovery-copy screenshots; uninstall surveys containing guilt language directed *at the app*.
