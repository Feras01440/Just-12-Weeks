# Age-Inclusive Use Evidence (Public Sources)

**Status:** Stage 1A synthesis — Q11 (accessibility and digital-confidence problems affecting older adults) with supporting tone evidence from §E2. Rows: [corpus](public-evidence-corpus.md) §E4 older-adult cluster (EV-4025…4043) + E2 tone rows. This synthesis carries direct design consequences for D-001. Interpretation labelled.

## Findings

1. **The 50+ market is equipped and blocked by usability, not motivation** *(verified survey fact)*: a 2026 national survey of adults 50+ reports near-universal device ownership with the named barriers being privacy (~1/3), ease of use (20%) and set-up/support (19%) (EV-4027). The audience D-001 protects exists at scale and is *interested*.
2. **Fear is concrete and quotable:** "I'm going to press the wrong button and I'm going to kill the phone" — cited in 25 of 34 interviews in a 2025 study of older learners (EV-4030, research-quoted). Older users also **blame themselves** for design failures (EV-4025, long-standing usability research) — so silent churn will understate every problem in our analytics. *(Consequence: per-band reporting everywhere; never read absence of complaints as absence of problems.)*
3. **Subscription anxiety is rational and worst for the oldest:** ~70 subscription complaints/day to the US regulator in 2024, up from 42/day in 2021; an 80-year-old's complaint — "I don't want to spend my remaining years fussing with companies over this" (EV-4028, EV-4029, verified). The click-to-cancel rule's vacatur means **app-level trust signals matter more, not less**. *(Confirms: renewal reminders, in-app cancellation walkthroughs (F06-profile idea from the panel), and the F20 store-billing distinction.)*
4. **Small text is a live recurring complaint across fitness ecosystems** (EV-4036…4038: three independent product-forum threads), compounding a 2026 research finding that **flat, text-free icons — the current default aesthetic — perform worst for older users**, with mobile navigation "a significant source of frustration" (EV-4026). *(Confirms: labels-always-with-icons, 200%-first layouts, no icon-only navigation.)*
5. **Condescension kills twice:** patronising coaching tone is a recurring exit reason among paying adults (EV-4031, EV-4032 + EV-2024); and explicit "senior modes" backfire — "a 70-year-old pictures a 'senior' as someone 90"; age-labelled simplification "signals that you view this demographic as less capable" (EV-4040, EV-4039). **Older users want good design, not "elderly design"** (EV-4041). *(Confirms D-001's framing: age-inclusive universal design, never a segregated mode; and gives the objection register's O-10 tone question its sharpest edge.)*
6. **What wins with older users:** consistency and clarity (the 60+ cohort holds the *longest* streaks of any age group on a major learning app — EV-4042, company-reported); clinician-backed purpose-built programmes (EV-4043); human instructors praised even while the surrounding app fails (EV-4035). **The single most damaging micro-failure found:** a seniors-only fitness app forcing daily re-login with no "remember me" (EV-4034) — authentication as an accessibility barrier.
7. **Digital-confidence design implications** *(professional interpretation over rows 2, 4, 6)*: forgiving error recovery and visible undo are not polish but adoption gates; "wrong button" fear means destructive actions need confirmation and *nothing* should be irreversible by one tap; predictable, stable layouts beat clever ones.

## What public evidence cannot answer here *(feeds research-limitations and D-009 triggers)*

Prevalence of these problems among the *audited habit apps'* older users specifically (store review pages unfetchable; no self-identified-age reviews retrievable) · our own product's performance for this audience (only Stage-5 testing with the mandated 65+ cohort answers that — and unmoderated methods cannot *diagnose* failures, per the unmoderated plan's escalation rule) · the 2026 icon study's sample/country detail (full text unreachable).

## Consequences registered

D-001's design requirements gain external evidence line-by-line (legibility, plain language, low cognitive burden, digital-confidence support). Additions queued from this evidence: "remember me"/long-session authentication as an explicit requirement (FR-03 note), in-app cancellation walkthrough with pictures (F18/FR-72 enhancement), and the per-band silent-churn caution written into the validation dashboard's reading rules.
