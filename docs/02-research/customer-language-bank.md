# Customer Language Bank

**Status:** Stage 1A instrument. Real users' words (as indexed; ≤25-word fair-quotation excerpts, corpus row refs attached) organised for product and copy work. **Usage rules:** these phrases inform *our understanding and testing stimuli*; they are never quoted in marketing as testimonials, never attributed to our product, and paraphrase-marked rows are not quotation candidates at all. The bank exists because the product's voice should answer the customer's voice — precisely.

## The pain, in their words

**On not knowing what to do:** "instead of scratching my head" (E2-41) · "I don't know how [to] ensure that a workout I would build is healthiest" (E2-43) · "so many workout apps, and they're all a bit confusing" (E2-44) · [para] beginners "fail because they don't know what to do" (E2-45).
**On logging burden:** "a slog to use" (E2-01) · [para] "6–10 taps for what took 2–3" (E2-02) · [para] "a 90-second routine to a 5-minute chore" (E2-04) · "Sometimes I feel like not logging things because I know it's really unhealthy" (E2-07).
**On streaks and lapses:** "like losing a limb" (E1-38) · "it hurts" (E1-39) · "Lost another streak...incredibly frustrated!" (E2-05) · "Now I feel worse, not better" (E2-16) · "A MEDITATION app was stressing me out. I uninstalled it." (E2-16b) · [para] "I've already failed, so what's the point of continuing?" (E1-37).
**On the metric replacing the goal:** "I was learning how to keep a streak alive" [not Spanish] (E1-12) · [para] still couldn't speak after 1,569 days (E1-11).
**On being patronised:** "as though I as a user were too stupid to understand" (E2-23) · "a stupidly vapid… rather condescending tone of voice" (E4-31) · [para] a senior is "someone 90" — age-modes rejected (E4-38).
**On billing betrayal:** "deceptive dark pattern design and outright fraud" (E3-09) · "MasterClass is an ANNUAL auto-renew subscription and they DO NOT refund anyone" (E3-14) · "I had no idea of it being an annual subscription" (E3-43) · "I don't want to spend my remaining years fussing with companies over this." (E4-28) · [para] "the quiz dumps users into a paywall" (E3-07).
**On digital fear:** "I'm going to press the wrong button and I'm going to kill the phone" (E4-30).

## The desire, in their words

**Guidance as relief:** "I feel safe and confident and don't have to think about the incremental steps" (E2-17) · "Is very calming to know what to do next" (E2-38) · "Adaptive Training removes any guess work and lets me just train" (E2-19) · "It's all taken care of… No thinking. Just work." (E2-37) · "I never have to think about what exercises I should do" (E2-36).
**Accountability as the paid thing:** "the only method I've ever found that has consistently gotten me in the gym… months in a row" (E3-29) · "the accountability keeps me consistent" (E3-30) · [para] "a good program that keeps you accountable and motivated is worth it" (E3-28).
**Forgiveness noticed:** "No guilt from the app if you go over" (E2-14) · [para] "the bird never dies" — support, not guilt, on missed days (E1-35).
**Finishing as identity:** "graduated and can now comfortably run 5k, or even 10k" (E3-38) · "This is the longest I have ever stuck with any exercise routine in my life" (E3-39b) · [para] first post: "too heavy to run?" → full 5K within months (E1-04).
**Premium, described:** "the UI is absolutely gorgeous" (E2-48) · "gentle (as promised), affirming, and quietly powerful" (E2-47) · "a delightful interface that never gets in the way" (E2-53) · "Satisfying to fill out, and beautiful to look at" (E2-51).

## Words the market taught users to distrust *(anti-lexicon input)*

"free trial" (heard as: card trap — E3-01…03) · "personalised" (heard as: quiz-to-paywall; generic output — E3-23, 24) · "coach" without a human (heard as: "a facade" — E2-32) · "limited time" (regulator-named manipulation) · "senior-friendly" (heard as: you think I'm less capable — E4-36) · "cancel anytime" unaccompanied by *how* (heard as: famous last words — E3-05, 15).

## How this bank is used

Copy testing stimuli (which phrasings our audience already carries) · interview/unmoderated probe wording (mirror their language, never our jargon) · content-strategy voice calibration (the product answers "scratching my head" with "know exactly what to do today" — the promise is literally the customer's sentence inverted) · claims-policy sensitivity (the distrusted-words list extends the banned lexicons contextually). Every entry keeps its row ref so provenance survives reuse.
