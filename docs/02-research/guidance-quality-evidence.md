**Status:** Stage 1A synthesis — Q3 (logging dislike), Q4 (guidance valuable vs controlling), Q7 (daily expectations), Q13 (premium qualities). Rows: [corpus](public-evidence-corpus.md) §E2 (63 data rows: EV-2001…EV-2063; count programmatically verified). ## Q3 — What users dislike about tracking and manual logging

1. **Tap-count is the currency of resentment.** The loudest 2026 complaint wave (a market-leading logger's redesign) is expressed in interaction units — "6–10 taps for what took 2–3", "a 90-second routine to a 5-minute chore" (EV-2002…2004, recurring review theme, high confidence). Users audit logging cost with precision; our G2 budget (≤3 taps median) is aimed at a *measured* pain.
2. **Streaks convert missed logging into felt failure** — 100/340-day logging streaks mourned; a 200-day meditation streak lost in hospital: "Now I feel worse, not better" (EV-2005, EV-2006, EV-2016). Logging streaks measure app attendance, not behaviour.
3. **Logging guilt produces data avoidance:** "Sometimes I feel like not logging things because I know it's really unhealthy" (EV-2007, quoted in review-mining research) — the tracker gets lied to before it gets deleted. Tone moderates everything: a no-judgement logger is praised precisely for "No guilt from the app if you go over" (EV-2014).
4. **The tracker becomes the chore:** tracked wants "suddenly seem like chores"; gamified maintenance yields "more screen time and less productivity"; a self-care app's early days read as a task pile (EV-2010…2013). Daily check-in tolerance is measured in seconds (EV-2012).
5. **All-or-nothing framing precedes quitting:** "When tools treat progress as all or nothing, people eventually choose nothing" (EV-2063, professional interpretation).

## Q4 — When guidance feels valuable rather than controlling

1. **Guidance is welcomed as safety at the moment of effort:** "I feel safe and confident and don't have to think about the incremental steps" (C25K, EV-2018, pinned verbatim); "very calming to know what to do next" (EV-2040). Being told what to do is experienced as *relief*, not submission — the single strongest external validation of D-002 captured.
2. **The valued object is a plan that has visibly thought about *me*:** adaptive plans ("removes any guess work and lets me just train", EV-2020) and a human watching (EV-2047) earn compliance; being "pushed like a real athlete" is loved where chosen (EV-2032).
3. **The same structure flips to control when it ignores state or removes choice:** the definitive natural experiment is one product's forced linear path — praised for killing decision fatigue *and* petitioned against by ~15,000 users as "extremely rigid" (EV-2026…2029). **Direction is loved; lock-in is not.**
4. **Fake personalisation is the fastest trust-killer:** "as though I were too stupid" (EV-2024); "any health coach that actually looked at my profile would respond differently" (EV-2025); a rules-engine sold as a coach is "a facade" (EV-2033).
5. **Context-blind nudges are guidance's most hated form:** stand reminders firing at night; move reminders that re-enable themselves (EV-2035, EV-2036) — users respond by disabling coaching wholesale.
6. **Rule the evidence supports:** *users grant an app authority in proportion to perceived listening.* Guidance that visibly adapts is "coaching"; guidance that doesn't is "bossiness" (synthesis over EV-2018…2036). Our adaptation-transparency design ("because you said last week felt heavy…") is aimed exactly here.

## Q7 — What users expect an app to tell them each day

1. **The demand is literal and daily:** "I never have to think about what exercises I should do" (EV-2037, pinned); "No thinking. Just work." (EV-2039); "so I know what to do when I get to gym instead of scratching my head" (EV-2042). The wanted deliverable is **today's concrete assignment, not a library**.
2. **Absence of direction is a named churn driver:** paying users complain "no specific instructions" (EV-2043); a 50-app audit concludes beginners "fail because they don't know what to do" (EV-2046, methodology unverified); category shoppers find "so many workout apps… all a bit confusing" (EV-2045).
3. **Users happily outsource sequencing across domains** — training splits, cycling blocks, even curricula — provided progression is visible and credible (EV-2020, EV-2026, EV-2041).
4. **Choice overload is the failure mode; a single prescriptive "today" is the praised pattern** — external confirmation of the Today-screen anatomy (one hero, nothing else).

## Q13 — What reads premium (and what reads cheap)

1. **The premium vocabulary in user language:** *clean, calm, beautiful, gorgeous, simple, fast, satisfying* (EV-2048, EV-2050, EV-2054, EV-2055, EV-2057). Premium = **the interface disappears during use** ("a delightful interface that never gets in the way", EV-2058).
2. **Micro-interactions carry outsized signal:** a matte-to-glossy completion animation is cited as why finishing feels rewarding (EV-2056); polish converts sceptics (EV-2051).
3. **Tone is treated as a design material:** copy that is "gentle (as promised), affirming, and quietly powerful" is counted as part of the premium feel (EV-2049) — the content-strategy voice is a *visual* asset in users' accounting.
4. **The cheap-signal list:** aggressive default notifications, widgets that fail, paywalls disproportionate to features ("scam like"), platform-inconsistent bugs at premium prices, generic algorithmic output ("first ten to fifteen workouts feel generic"), redesigns that add taps (EV-2053, EV-2059…2061). Clutter and friction — not visuals alone — read as cheap.

## Design consequences registered

External confirmation for: D-002's core bet (guidance-as-relief), the ≤3-tap loop, adaptation transparency, no context-blind nudges (our closed notification set), the calm-premium aesthetic direction, and the anti-generic mandate. New nuance adopted: the **lock-in caveat** — our guidance must keep visible user control (variant lanes, declinable adaptations already designed; objection O-09/O-10 track the residual risks).
