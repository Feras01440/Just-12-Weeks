# Guidance Quality Evidence (Public User-Voice)

**Status:** Stage 1A synthesis — Q3 (logging dislike), Q4 (guidance valuable vs controlling), Q7 (daily expectations), Q13 (premium qualities). Rows: [corpus](public-evidence-corpus.md) §E2 (58 rows). All interpretation labelled; limitations per [research-limitations.md](research-limitations.md).

## Q3 — What users dislike about tracking and manual logging

1. **Tap-count is the currency of resentment.** The loudest 2026 complaint wave (a market-leading logger's redesign) is expressed in interaction units — "6–10 taps for what took 2–3", "a 90-second routine to a 5-minute chore" (E2-02…04, recurring review theme, high confidence). Users audit logging cost with precision; our G2 budget (≤3 taps median) is aimed at a *measured* pain.
2. **Streaks convert missed logging into felt failure** — 100/340-day logging streaks mourned; a 200-day meditation streak lost in hospital: "Now I feel worse, not better" (E2-05, 06, 16). Logging streaks measure app attendance, not behaviour.
3. **Logging guilt produces data avoidance:** "Sometimes I feel like not logging things because I know it's really unhealthy" (E2-07, quoted in review-mining research) — the tracker gets lied to before it gets deleted. Tone moderates everything: a no-judgement logger is praised precisely for "No guilt from the app if you go over" (E2-14).
4. **The tracker becomes the chore:** tracked wants "suddenly seem like chores"; gamified maintenance yields "more screen time and less productivity"; a self-care app's early days read as a task pile (E2-10…13). Daily check-in tolerance is measured in seconds (E2-12).
5. **All-or-nothing framing precedes quitting:** "When tools treat progress as all or nothing, people eventually choose nothing" (E2-58, professional interpretation).

## Q4 — When guidance feels valuable rather than controlling

1. **Guidance is welcomed as safety at the moment of effort:** "I feel safe and confident and don't have to think about the incremental steps" (C25K, E2-17, pinned verbatim); "very calming to know what to do next" (E2-38). Being told what to do is experienced as *relief*, not submission — the single strongest external validation of D-002 captured.
2. **The valued object is a plan that has visibly thought about *me*:** adaptive plans ("removes any guess work and lets me just train", E2-19) and a human watching (E2-44) earn compliance; being "pushed like a real athlete" is loved where chosen (E2-31).
3. **The same structure flips to control when it ignores state or removes choice:** the definitive natural experiment is one product's forced linear path — praised for killing decision fatigue *and* petitioned against by ~15,000 users as "extremely rigid" (E2-25…28). **Direction is loved; lock-in is not.**
4. **Fake personalisation is the fastest trust-killer:** "as though I were too stupid" (E2-23); "any health coach that actually looked at my profile would respond differently" (E2-24); a rules-engine sold as a coach is "a facade" (E2-32).
5. **Context-blind nudges are guidance's most hated form:** stand reminders firing at night; move reminders that re-enable themselves (E2-34, 35) — users respond by disabling coaching wholesale.
6. **Rule the evidence supports:** *users grant an app authority in proportion to perceived listening.* Guidance that visibly adapts is "coaching"; guidance that doesn't is "bossiness" (synthesis over E2-17…35). Our adaptation-transparency design ("because you said last week felt heavy…") is aimed exactly here.

## Q7 — What users expect an app to tell them each day

1. **The demand is literal and daily:** "I never have to think about what exercises I should do" (E2-36, pinned); "No thinking. Just work." (E2-38); "so I know what to do when I get to gym instead of scratching my head" (E2-41). The wanted deliverable is **today's concrete assignment, not a library**.
2. **Absence of direction is a named churn driver:** paying users complain "no specific instructions" (E2-42); a 50-app audit concludes beginners "fail because they don't know what to do" (E2-45, methodology unverified); category shoppers find "so many workout apps… all a bit confusing" (E2-44).
3. **Users happily outsource sequencing across domains** — training splits, cycling blocks, even curricula — provided progression is visible and credible (E2-19, 25, 40).
4. **Choice overload is the failure mode; a single prescriptive "today" is the praised pattern** — external confirmation of the Today-screen anatomy (one hero, nothing else).

## Q13 — What reads premium (and what reads cheap)

1. **The premium vocabulary in user language:** *clean, calm, beautiful, gorgeous, simple, fast, satisfying* (E2-46…51). Premium = **the interface disappears during use** ("a delightful interface that never gets in the way", E2-53).
2. **Micro-interactions carry outsized signal:** a matte-to-glossy completion animation is cited as why finishing feels rewarding (E2-52); polish converts sceptics (E2-49).
3. **Tone is treated as a design material:** copy that is "gentle (as promised), affirming, and quietly powerful" is counted as part of the premium feel (E2-47) — the content-strategy voice is a *visual* asset in users' accounting.
4. **The cheap-signal list:** aggressive default notifications, widgets that fail, paywalls disproportionate to features ("scam like"), platform-inconsistent bugs at premium prices, generic algorithmic output ("first ten to fifteen workouts feel generic"), redesigns that add taps (E2-54…57). Clutter and friction — not visuals alone — read as cheap.

## Design consequences registered

External confirmation for: D-002's core bet (guidance-as-relief), the ≤3-tap loop, adaptation transparency, no context-blind nudges (our closed notification set), the calm-premium aesthetic direction, and the anti-generic mandate. New nuance adopted: the **lock-in caveat** — our guidance must keep visible user control (variant lanes, declinable adaptations already designed; objection O-09/O-10 track the residual risks).
