# Product Thesis

**Status:** Draft for founder review. Labels per decision-log convention.

## The problem *(Evidence-supported inference)*

Adults repeatedly commit to meaningful goals whose results take longer to appear than motivation lasts unaided. They abandon not for lack of desire but because: (1) nobody tells them exactly what to do today and how; (2) progress is invisible during the lag before results; (3) one disrupted week converts into total collapse because nothing is designed for the return. Existing tools are trackers — they record effort instead of directing it, and their engagement mechanics (streaks, guilt notifications) actively worsen the collapse moment.

## The solution

A guided 12-week programme platform. The user picks one meaningful goal; the app delivers a professionally authored, evidence-based programme as **daily guided actions** — tell, show, guide, adapt (D-002) — with weekly structure, milestone checkpoints, programme-appropriate progress evidence (D-003), designed recovery after lapses, and a completion experience that proves the twelve weeks happened.

## Target customer *(Unvalidated hypothesis — Stage 1)*

Behaviourally defined (D-001): adults 18+ with a meaningful goal requiring sustained effort who need structured guidance, encouragement and recovery support. First validation emphasis: **Pattern A, "The Restarter"** — the serial sincere attempter whose defining failure is the post-disruption collapse.

## Jobs the product performs

- **Emotional job:** "Carry my belief through the weeks when I can't see results yet — and don't make me feel like a failure when life interrupts."
- **Functional job:** "Decide what I should do today, show me how to do it properly, adjust when I can't, and prove to me that I'm progressing."

## Why users choose it *(hypotheses to validate)*

Because it answers "what do I do today and how?" instead of "what did you log today?"; because it is visibly built on real evidence with named review; because it promises — structurally, not rhetorically — that missing days is survivable.

## Why users stay

Within a cycle: daily guidance keeps tomorrow concrete; weekly reviews convert effort into visible progress; recovery design converts the historical quit-moment into a return-moment. Across cycles: completion hands the user their next meaningful goal (week-13 transition, Q13) on a platform whose breadth (D-004) makes "next" credible.

## Why users pay

They are buying a **programme and a guide** — the quality of authored content, instruction and adaptation — not a logging utility. The category's paying users already buy structure (memberships, courses, coaching) at prices far above app subscriptions; the product's price anchors against those, not against free trackers.

## Defensible advantage *(honest assessment)*

Near-term: execution quality — the guidance-first experience, recovery design, and governance-backed content are all rare in combination, but each is imitable alone. Durable: (1) a programme engine + governance system that makes each additional expert-reviewed programme cheaper and safer to ship than competitors can match ad hoc; (2) a brand that owns "actually finishing"; (3) completion-report evidence corpus (consented) that no streak app can counterfeit. There is no network-effect moat; the defence is compounding content quality and trust.

## Likely failure modes *(from Annex C and synthesis §7)*

Week-13 churn cliff; paywall-before-value activation collapse; content cost underestimation → shallow programmes → promise falsified; generic-tracker perception; measurement credibility failure; solo-founder capacity. Each has a named mitigation in the [risk register](../10-roadmap/risk-register.md).

## Thesis statement

> **Adults finish meaningful goals when a trustworthy guide converts the goal into today's action, makes progress visible before results are, and designs the comeback instead of punishing the lapse. A premium, evidence-based, guidance-first 12-week platform can own "actually finishing" — a position the tracker category structurally cannot copy without ceasing to be trackers — and can monetise honestly because finishing is worth paying for.**

Falsifiable via Stage 1 signals (research plan §9–10): if target users don't recognise the abandonment-before-results problem, won't pay for structure, or experience the 12-week frame as pressure, the thesis fails and we stop or pivot.
