## 4. The strongest product opportunity *(evidence-supported inference)*

Across 31 audited competitor products, the guided middle — *"software that tells me what to do today, notices me, adapts, and gets me to a defined finish"* — is occupied only by humans at £900–2,400/year or by single-domain programmes. **Recovery-as-content exists nowhere in the studied market**; billing conduct is the category's dominant complaint; no product surfaces authorship, review or citations; none documents accessibility. Your product's four differentiators (guidance-first, designed recovery, governed evidence, honest term pricing) each map onto a verified market gap, and the behavioural-science evidence base supports the mechanism design point by point.

## 5. The greatest risks *(ranked; register: [risk-register.md](../10-roadmap/risk-register.md))*

1. **Week-13 economics** (R-01): the promise ends; the business must not — the quarterly-term + next-journey design is the structural answer, unproven until beta.
2. **Content cost** (R-03): one genuinely excellent, reviewer-signed programme is 6–10 FTE-weeks plus review cycles; this is where budgets die quietly. The authoring pilot before full build is the tripwire.

4. **Real-world attrition** (R-05): ~3–4% 30-day retention is the category's gravity; recovery design is the bet against it.
5. **Founder capacity** (R-06): everything above is sized against Q9 answers that don't exist yet.
