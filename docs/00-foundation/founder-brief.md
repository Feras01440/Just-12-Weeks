Transcription conventions: spelling is normalised for readability; wording and meaning are preserved. Anything uncertain in the handwriting is marked `[?]`. Sketches are described in square brackets.

## Page 1

Plain light-blue cover page. No content.

## Page 2 — The core idea

> [Sketch: a yellow rounded square containing a hand-drawn "12" — an early app-icon idea]
>
> **The 12 weeks challenge!**
>
> Can be used to track building habits or changing important things in our life that might take **time** to see actual results — like body weight (losing weight, gaining and building muscles), whitening teeth, skin care, or learning an actual skill that takes time, like coding, etc.

Note: "time" is underlined in the original — the founder emphasises that these are goals whose results are inherently slow to appear.

## Page 3 — Design quality and first launch

> The design and user interface are the most important part of the app. Being simple, having fancy `[?]` colours and smooth. **Sleep Cycle** as an example of ease of use, way of design and premium interface.
>
> [Sketch: a phone screen with a large graphic, several lines of text, and a "Continue" button]
>
> When a new user opens the app, a page will appear showing in an exciting and engaging way the importance of the app.

Note: the word transcribed as "fancy" is ambiguous in the handwriting (possibly "fun" or "fine"). The intent — distinctive, pleasing colour — is clear. "Importance of the app" is underlined in red in the original.

## Page 4 — The offer page

> **An offer page** appears after pressing Continue, to convince the user to pay for a one-month or 12-months subscription at a reduced price. Attempts to show the user that this would give them full access to all features, including cosmetic and important features. The offer only lasts for *n* hours, and then it goes back to the normal price.

Note: "offer page" is written in red in the original. The "n hours" is literal — the founder left the duration as a variable.

## Page 5 — Recommended plans

> The app should contain at least 12 recommended plans to do — like losing weight — with 3D designs and interactive proven ways to track for everything. Then other 12 needed list like this: `[?]`
>
> [Sketch: a phone screen titled "recommended" showing a grid of six plan cards; the first two are labelled "lose weight" and "skin care"]

Note: the sentence "Then other 12 needed list like this" is ambiguous. Plausible readings: (a) a further list of 12 user-requested/custom plans alongside the 12 recommended ones, or (b) simply "the other plans needed, listed like this [sketch]". This requires founder clarification — see [assumptions-and-questions.md](assumptions-and-questions.md), question Q4.

## Page 6 — Evidence-based content

> The app should be based on books and researches and proven techniques, to help and explain it really well to the customer.
