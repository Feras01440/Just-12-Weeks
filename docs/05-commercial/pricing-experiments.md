# Pricing Experiments

**Status:** Experiment plan — prices below are **hypotheses in GBP, explicitly unvalidated** (founder brief forbids pretending otherwise). Sequenced from cheap evidence to expensive evidence. Every experiment: pre-registered hypothesis, primary metric, guardrails (refund rate, rating, support volume), and a decision it feeds. Ethics rail: no experiment ships a pattern paywall-principles bans; no experiments on recovery/safety surfaces.

## Phase A — before any code (Stage 1, weeks not months)

**A1. Interview price anchoring (no hypotheticals).** In F-section of interviews: past spend reconstruction (what they actually paid for structure: PT blocks, courses, apps) → establishes each pattern's lived price band. Output: anchor distribution per behavioural pattern. Decision fed: which models (monetisation M1–M8) are inside lived bands.

**A2. Landing-page price framing test.** Same promise, three framings across variants: £24.99 one-off programme / £19.99 per 12-week term / £7.99 monthly. Measure: email-signup rate per arm + a one-question "what would you expect this to include?" follow-up. Weak signal, honest floor-check only. Decision: kills any framing that craters interest or badly mismatches expectations.

**A3. Van Westendorp add-on (n≥60 from waitlist).** Four-question price sensitivity on the winning framing. Output: acceptable range brackets for Phase B arms. (Treated as directional — stated-preference caveat applied.)

## Phase B — prototype/beta (Stages 5, 9)

**B1. Paywall comprehension (not price) test.** Stage 5: G9 bar — 5/5 participants state price, period, renewal, cancellation unaided, across age cohorts, at 200% text. Runs on every candidate paywall before any conversion test. A paywall that converts but fails comprehension is discarded (that's the whole point of this company).

**B2. Boundary timing test (beta cohorts).** Q6 candidates: (a) hard boundary post-suitability vs (b) free week 1 then boundary vs (c) free days 1–3. Primary: D30 revenue per install; guardrails: refund rate, D7 retention delta, review sentiment. Hypothesis (from category evidence): (b) wins on blended value. Decision: Q6.

**B3. Unit test (beta, if volume allows; else staged post-launch).** £19.99/12-week term vs £7.99/monthly vs £44.99/annual (catalogue-honesty gate applies to annual arm). Primary: 6-month realized revenue per starter + week-13 continuation rate; guardrails as ever. Decision: Q5's model centre of gravity.

## Phase C — post-launch (Stage 12, sequential not simultaneous)

**C1. Intro-offer honesty test.** No-intro vs genuine one-time first-term intro (e.g. first 12-week term £14.99, standard £19.99 stated adjacent, never re-offered, store-native intro pricing). Measure: conversion lift vs refund/trust guardrails. Any arm resembling countdown mechanics is constitutionally out (Q5).
**C2. Trial-with-reminder test.** M8 reminder-first trial vs no-trial direct. Measure: trial→paid, 6-month realized revenue, "unexpected charge" support tickets (must be ~zero).
**C3. Programme-purchase price points.** M1 at £24.99 vs £34.99 with identical framing (catalogue ≥2 required). Measure: revenue per visitor, completion-rate parity (price shouldn't select for completion-hostile buyers).
**C4. Committed-monthly annual** (non-US/SG stores only†): annual-value-at-monthly-cashflow arm once annual exists.

## Standing rules

One pricing experiment live at a time (sequential inference over simultaneous noise at our volumes); minimum detectable effect sized before launch (no peeking-until-significant); every result — including nulls — logged here with dates and fed to the decision log; grandfathering policy: early buyers keep their terms forever (price changes never claw back); no personalised pricing, ever (same price for the same thing for everyone in a storefront — both an ethics line and a DMCC-era safety).

## What this plan refuses to learn

Whether desperation-timing (paywalling the recovery conversation), guilt discounts ("come back — 50% off"), or obfuscated renewals would lift revenue. They would. We are not building that company — and per the market audit (T1), that revenue is borrowed from a trust balance that, once spent, prices the whole brand at zero.
