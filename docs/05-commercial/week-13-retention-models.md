# Week-13 Retention Models

**Status:** Options analysis for Q13 (**Founder decision required**). The structural problem: the promise honestly *ends* at week 12; the business needs a reason to exist on week 13 that doesn't corrupt the ending. Evidence frame: 95% of cancelled annual subscribers never return†; median first annual renewals 23–40%†; the completion moment is simultaneously peak goodwill and peak "done-ness".

## The four models (composable, not exclusive)

### W1 — Next-journey sequencing ("your next 12 weeks")
The completion report hands over to a guided next-goal selection (F23): different category welcome (the platform claim, D-004, becomes the retention engine), same-programme cycle-2 where programmes support it (progression variants).
- **User value:** high — multi-goal lives are real (Pattern A's defining trait); the fresh-start moment is behaviourally right for a new commitment.
- **Revenue:** the honest subscription justification; with M3 quarterly, renewal *is* the next-journey decision — incentives fully aligned.
- **Requirements:** ≥2 credible programmes (Q3/Q9 dependency) or cycle-2 depth in the flagship; guided-selection UX; the report's transition designed (done — F23).
- **Risks:** catalogue-of-one weakens it at launch (mitigation: cycle-2 + vote-for-next demand capture); pushiness would poison the completion moment (mitigation: F23's equal-dignity paths).

### W2 — Maintenance mode ("keep what you built")
A lighter authored continuation: 2–3 guided touchpoints/week consolidating the flagship's outcome (habit-maintenance evidence frame; the practice getting self-sustaining is the *honest* goal).
- **User value:** high for the "I don't want to lose this" cohort; directly answers the post-programme cliff the fitness industry never solved.
- **Revenue:** weaker alone (maintenance feels cheaper than transformation); natural as an *included* bridge inside a term/subscription rather than a paid product.
- **Requirements:** authored maintenance content per programme (template §10); modest.
- **Risks:** maintenance-forever becomes tracker-ish drift (D-002 watch); scope it as a bridge (4–6 weeks) into W1, not a destination.

### W3 — Alumni identity ("the shelf")
The permanent, growing shelf of finished journeys (week-12 spec §5): identity-based retention — people return to the place that holds their proof. Cohort/community layers (N-20) would extend this post-MVP.
- **User value:** quiet but real; costs the user nothing.
- **Revenue:** indirect (return-visit surface for W1 moments; word-of-mouth via honest share cards).
- **Requirements:** already designed (reports permanent, F18 no-hostage rule keeps the shelf reachable even unentitled — which *is* the point: the shelf brings them back honestly).
- **Risks:** none material. This is free retention hygiene and is effectively already decided by existing specs — listed for completeness.

### W4 — Accept single-cycle economics (price the ending honestly)
M1 programme-purchase primacy: sell complete journeys, expect many customers to buy one, price accordingly (£29–39/programme hypothesis), treat repeat buyers as upside not plan.
- **User value:** maximal honesty; zero retention pressure anywhere in the product.
- **Revenue:** lowest ceiling; every quarter restarts at zero; CAC must be recovered per single purchase — hard maths at consumer price points.
- **Requirements:** none beyond M1. **Risks:** structurally caps the business; makes content investment (R-03) harder to recover; the founder's platform vision (D-004) under-monetised.

## Professional recommendation *(reversible, unapproved)*

**W1 as the spine, W2 as the built-in bridge, W3 always** — expressed commercially as the M3 quarterly term whose renewal moment *is* the week-13 conversation. W4 remains the honest fallback if Stage-1/beta evidence shows multi-cycle appetite is weak (in which case pricing moves toward M1 and the company plans as a catalogue business, not a subscription one). The decisive evidence: `next_journey_selected` ≥30% in beta (success-metrics) and the Q3 second-programme feasibility answer. Decision lands in the decision log at Gate 2.
